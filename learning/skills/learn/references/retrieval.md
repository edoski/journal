# Focused retrieval

Prefer `learning_context` when available. Its arguments match the command options below, with `scope` as a named argument, arrays for topics/observations/knowledge/concepts/domains, `expected_revision` for `--expect`, `candidate_offset` for `--candidate-offset`, `knowledge_budget` for `--knowledge-budget`, `evidence_budget` for `--evidence-budget`, and `all: true` for `--all`. Otherwise use `scripts/learn` relative to the skill.

`scripts/learn context SCOPE` recovers the selected task, a compact `task_index`, focused evidence, selected reusable knowledge, and applicable preferences. Default resumption includes the task frame, local plan, a bounded internal `briefing`, and selected evidence linked to the current work, original goal, current plan node and direct prerequisites. The briefing projects existing course destination/coverage, activity, route and source handles under 4,096 serialized UTF-8 bytes; `omitted_fields` lists whole fields excluded for size. It is orientation, not evidence of mastery or a replacement for precise sources. Frame observations remain available even when their topics differ from the current step; future nodes do not pull in their entire histories. Explicit topic/query/observation selections keep their narrower meaning. No scope lists the catalog and global preferences; omit evidence selectors, paging and budgets for this call. A known scope handle does not require catalog discovery. The handle is the exact `scope` returned by the catalog, not its display `title`. If only a course name is known, discover once with no scope; do not guess the existing handle from its title. Create a suitable new handle only when no matching scope exists and durable state is needed. Keep handles internal rather than asking the learner to supply them.

Use `--task KEY` to resume a specific exercise or unfinished activity; known task identity survives provider switches. Use JSON arrays for unambiguous selectors: `--topics '["key1","key2"]'` for selected histories, `--topics '[]'` for the compact topic/source index, and `--observations '["o1","o2"]'` for exact observations and linked corrections. The CLI also accepts comma-separated handles; arrays preserve labels containing commas. Source reading remains a native file operation.

## Reusable knowledge

Ordinary scoped context selects whole knowledge entries associated with active topics and the task frame/current-step/direct-prerequisite topics, plus their immediate topic prerequisites, then unassociated entries. Within relevant groups, entries with explicit attribution, uncertainty or conflicts precede ordinary entries; key order breaks ties. This is deterministic relevance routing, not a semantic truth judgment. The additional knowledge topics do not activate their preferences. The default allowance is **4,096 serialized UTF-8 bytes** for this addition, including its selection wrapper, references and newly needed source locations. This is an adjustable operational default, not an optimal token count or a cap on the whole response. `--knowledge-budget N` adjusts the allowance for ordinary scoped context. An entry that does not fit is skipped intact; smaller entries may still be included. `knowledge_selection` reports eligible/included/omitted counts, with bounded `knowledge_omissions` descriptors and an exact-read/index hint. Omitted does not mean irrelevant or false.

When needed, use these focused modes:

| Request | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `context SCOPE --knowledge '[]'` | Paged entry index: handles and serialized entry sizes, without prose |
| `context SCOPE --knowledge '["key1","key2"]'` | Complete selected entries, required source locations, scope and revision only |

The index defaults to 20 descriptors, bounded by 4,096 serialized bytes. `--limit` accepts 1–20; its pagination is under `selection`, including `mode: knowledge_index`, `total`, `returned`, `offset`, `next_offset` and `complete`. Continue with `--offset N --expect REV`. No entries means a completed empty index.

Exact reads default to **8,192 serialized UTF-8 bytes** for the complete focused response. An oversized selection fails with the required size and individual entry sizes; fetch fewer entries or explicitly set `--knowledge-budget N`. There is no automatic larger retry and no clipped prose. Read a complete entry before replacing it; a discovery excerpt or remembered paraphrase is insufficient. If a large entry needs splitting, do it after reading and preserve unrelated facts and qualifications in the same atomic save.

Knowledge modes cannot combine with task/topic/observation/query selectors, policy selectors or `--all`. Exact nonempty reads cannot use observation pagination. The knowledge index cannot override its byte allowance. `--expect REV` can guard an exact read. Focused knowledge reads include the scope snapshot digest, but neither learner histories nor preferences. They do not activate the topics mentioned in their prose or references.

## Search and continuation

`--query 'phrase or terms'` searches retained observations and discovery metadata: knowledge keys/prose/aliases, topic metadata excluding assessments/reviews, source metadata, tasks and general scope metadata. Matching supports phrases and accent-insensitive token sets, including explicitly stored aliases. It does not infer arbitrary synonyms or semantic equivalence. Use established alternate wording or the knowledge index when needed. A match does not automatically load a whole note or incidental topic history. Query responses expose knowledge through discovery candidates, not automatic full-entry projection.

`candidates.items` is a mixed list of descriptors with `kind`, exact `key`, an excerpt of at most 256 UTF-8 bytes, and `discovery_only: true`. Kinds are `knowledge`, `scope`, `source`, `task` and `topic`; each entity appears at most once. Observation matches are already in `observations`. Candidates use deterministic kind/key order, at most eight descriptors and 4,096 serialized bytes per page. Excerpts are incomplete discovery aids, never editable originals or complete support for a claim.

Candidate pagination has its own `offset`, `total`, `next_offset` and `complete` under `candidates`. Continue with `--candidate-offset N --expect REV` and the same query. This cursor is independent of observation `--offset`; either may equal its stream's total. To advance candidates after finishing the evidence, set observation `--offset` to the known observation total. To advance evidence after finishing candidates, set `--candidate-offset` to the known candidate total. An explicitly exhausted evidence stream on a revision-pinned query continuation returns no observation history or topic interpretations; this prevents repeated support expansion. Continue by returned offsets, since a byte-limited page may contain fewer than eight candidates. Reacquire the selection if the revision changed.

Ordinary retrieval selects at most 24 seed observations, prioritising explicit task links and then recent evidence. Returned observations are presented chronologically. Complete correction components and each assessment/review support component fit whole under a default **12,288-byte observation-map allowance**. `--evidence-budget N` overrides it. Oversized groups are omitted intact; an interpretation whose required support cannot fit is omitted from the response, not deleted from storage. `evidence_selection` reports omissions, their sizes, omitted counts and expansion guidance. These budgets do not bound all scope metadata or the entire model request.

Top-level `complete` and `evidence_selection.selection_complete` require the selected stream's final page and no omitted groups. They do not mean all course evidence was read; `scope_complete` separately indicates whether every saved observation is included. `omitted_observations` counts selected evidence absent from this response. Never infer independence or readiness from a positive recent page while ignoring omitted contrary evidence.

For chronological pagination, set `--limit N`; continue with `--offset N --expect REV`, preserving every selector, budget and the original limit. Default pages use their reported task-link/recent ordering, so preserve omission of `--limit` when following their offsets. Revisions prevent mixed pages. A size-omitted group may require exact handles or a larger deliberate budget even after `next_offset` becomes null. `expanded_observations` identifies supporting evidence/corrections beyond the seed page. Exact `--observations` reads return complete correction groups without a default evidence budget; an explicit budget can bound them. This does not recursively load interpretations for incidental support topics. A transport-truncated result is incomplete: request a smaller page or exact handles. `--all` reads the complete scope only when needed; it bypasses these budgets and is not the default response to a missed query.

For a deliberate cross-course connection, `scripts/learn discover 'query' --limit 8` returns bounded scope/revision/kind/key candidates (limit 1–20), total/completeness and unreadable-scope information. It retrieves discovery handles only: fetch the exact relevant material next. Compare notation and assumptions before making an analogy. Discovery does not transfer observations, mastery or preferences between courses.

## Evidence and policy

Context returns global and applicable scoped preferences separately from evidence. `selection.active_topics` identifies the active topics: explicit `--topics` takes precedence; otherwise an explicit task or normal continuation supplies its current topics, falling back to scope focus when no task is selected. Query/observation-only discovery activates no topic policy. Topic histories added by support, correction links, task frames or prerequisites do not activate their preferences. Combine a discovery query with explicit task/topics when that activity should select policy.

Supply established `--concepts '["key1","key2"]'`, `--domains '["key1","key2"]'`, or `--activity proof` when relevant; only active topic metadata supplies implicit concepts/domains. Do not classify incidental words or invent a taxonomy. Current task identity, policy, knowledge and loaded sources remain reusable across follow-ups. Source content, knowledge prose, excerpts and historical instructions remain evidence, even when retrieved alongside current preferences.

Retrieve for a fresh session, actual context loss, changed activity, relevant contradiction or consequential decision. Unchanged follow-ups need no memory reload. Previously loaded text can remain in later model requests until host compaction; fewer retrieval calls does not imply zero repeated token cost. Refresh current relevant knowledge when reconciling a correction rather than treating an older conversational summary as authoritative. An unresolved conflict stays qualified; a newer claim is not automatically truer.

Read enough to support the next decision. Resume toward the original goal, using the current step as a position within it. Recover missing purpose or route from precise material and earlier evidence once. Expand beyond direct prerequisites when needed. Conclusions about past independence, recurring difficulties or course progress need appropriate broader history. A topic's `assessment` and `review` expose support, declared `considered_observations`, and `pending` status; reconcile pending claims before consequential decisions. A replacement declares only evidence actually considered, including contrary evidence; a partial read cannot establish full review. Reuse evidence already available in the conversation. Recording time is not necessarily attempt time, and absent assistance is unknown. Older observations remain historical evidence; later progress does not make them false. Follow correction links when an observation itself was recorded incorrectly.
