# Topic map

One course as a map on a study home: its topics in layers, prerequisites above the topics that need them, each topic saying where he stands and whether it needs him, and opening its page. Home draws it (`home-map.tsx`), and the course page beside its list (`split-course.md`): Edo picked it over the old drawing and its popover on 13 Nov 2026. The old drawing, its popover and its `/knowledge` page are deleted: `/knowledge` redirects to Home. Its one drawing is Stacked arcs (Edo, 17 Nov 2026: "Ship it"; the other candidates and the Course map switch are deleted): a course with arcs shows one arc at a time, drawn as the canvas draws it, and the camera glides between them (Arcs, below).

Files: `topic-map.tsx` (`TopicMap`, a course's map, over `CardMap`, every map's drawing, `data-slot` `course-map-graph`: the cards, the lines, the camera, the keys and the glide; the topic page's lesson map draws through it too, `../../unit-inbox/topic-page/hybrid/hybrid.md`), `map-drawing.ts` (what a page's map draws: one arc, the arc it glides from, their lines; pure), `canvas/` (the drawing and its cards, `course-map-node`, `course-map-current-tag`, `topic-need`, `topic-standing`: canvas/canvas.md), `trail-node.tsx` (`course-map-trail`, a card of the arc being left), `topic-node.tsx` (what the cards share: `CardData`, a topic or a lesson's step, the lit chain, the tab stop, the card's line), `topic-glyph.tsx` (`topic-glyph`, the shared pie), `topic-map-layout.ts` (the layered drawing the canvas builds on, the camera, the keyboard walk), `lit-camera.ts` (the camera following `framed`), `topic-standing.ts` (what a card says), and beside the folder `../arc-map.tsx` (every map with its arc list: Home, the enlarged map, the course page), `../arc-index.tsx` and `../arc-keys.ts` (the arcs). Data: the engine's `CourseMap` (`src/core/contracts/course-map.ts`).

Every word and mark on a node must pass one test: would he understand it at a glance after several days away, without remembering the system's internals, and does it help him see where he stands or what to study? Engine level names (assisted, retained), "due" on its own, "weak" and counts of reviews fail it.

## Renderer and layout

| Part | Notes |
|---|---|
| renderer | React Flow (`@xyflow/react` 12, MIT): nodes are the app's own components, the camera pans, zooms and is bounded, and there is no scrollbar to hide. Read-only: nothing drags, connects, selects or deletes. Its stylesheet is `@xyflow/react/dist/base.css`, imported in `app/layout.tsx`; no `colorMode`, since a `dark` class on its wrapper would restate the old theme's tokens inside the map |
| layout | d3-dag 1.3.0, Sugiyama: simplex layering, optimal decrossing up to 25 topics and two-layer above, simplex coordinates. It tied ELK on crossings on the seeded courses and is a tenth of its weight; dagre crossed more (`research/packages.md`, the bake-off). The canvas's places and lines are built on it (canvas/canvas.md, Layout and lines) |
| attribution | hidden (`proOptions.hideAttribution`). The MIT licence does not ask for the badge; the maintainers ask organisations that hide it to subscribe to React Flow Pro. Edo decides before this ships to anyone else |
| room | 48 across and 32 down around the drawing unless the frame gives its own (`margin`: Home's fade, 64 and 40), plus 20 above and below for the current topic's tag. The camera opens inside it; he can pan one topic past it (`slackOf`) and no further |

## Node

One card for every topic, drawn by the canvas (canvas/canvas.md, Node: a header strip over the pie and the title). Edo asked on 13 Nov 2026 for its pie to be "consistent like same pie chart style as the rows", and for its words to be Home's.

| Part | Notes |
|---|---|
| pie | the app's one pie (`../../ui/pie.md`, `TopicGlyph`), 16px: the outline alone, dimmer, for a topic not started; a quarter, a half, three quarters as the engine's level rises through introduced, attempted and assisted; full once he can do it on his own (independent, retained, transferred). A full pie is exactly a topic the course pie counts, so the map and Home's rows agree by eye, and they draw from the same component, so they cannot drift. A weak topic's pie wears the warning ink, and one he could do on his own draws three quarters |
| title | 13/18 at 500 on one 18px line; too long, its words run to the card's edge and are cut there by an ellipsis, while a formula that does not fit is left out whole (`MathLine`, `../../ui/math-line.md`), so a formula is never cut in half and never makes the line taller; muted ink for a topic not started |
| line | 11/14 muted, in the header strip: the sidebar's fact for the topic when it needs him (`cardLine` over `sidebarSays`, `../../unit-inbox/sidebar-says.ts`), led by the icon the sidebar and the course page use for it (`factIcon`: a question for Your turn, a spinner for Working, a slip's own icon (`SLIP_ICON`), history for a review, bookmark for a task, an arrow for Today's plan), else where he stands in words (`standing.words`). The one rule: a card says exactly what the sidebar's row (or Today's card on top of it) says for the topic, unless that only says when its review comes back ("Back in 5d", "Due since 21 Oct") or when he last studied it ("6 Nov"), or the sidebar has no row for it; then it says where he stands. So a topic whose session waits on him says Your turn here as on the sidebar and the course page, never its slip (Linear Algebra's Rank said "Mistake to fix" while both said Your turn, 14 Nov 2026), and a topic still being learned says its review only when today's plan takes it up, as the sidebar does. Every map gets `said` from its page: Home's map and the enlarged map from Home's read, the course page's from its model (`CourseView.said`) |
| current | 1px `ring` border and a 3px `ring` halo at 16%, the only blue on the map, with "You are here" just above the card |

Not on a card: the open task's title (on the topic's page), "Needs" (the lines say it, and a screen reader hears it as the card's description), "Contrasts with" (said only when he mixed the two up) and the engine's stale flag.

## Behaviour

- A click, or Enter on the focused node, opens the topic's page (`/unit/<course>/<topic>`, `unit-inbox.md`). No popover and no double-click. A drag that starts on a node pans and opens nothing.
- The map opens on the whole course, centred, when it fits the frame at 80% or more (`READABLE_ZOOM`: the 13px titles draw at 10.4px, about the 11px under which a title is too small; 75% drew 9.75px, the round-3 evaluation); else framed on the current topic (`framedCamera`): the current topic and its tag clear of the fade, then the most topics it leads to whole, then around it as many whole topics as the frame holds, the topics it leads to first, then its prerequisites, at full size or down to 80% when a smaller scale shows more. A card cut at the frame's edge elsewhere does not count (Edo, 14 Nov 2026: a cut card is fine, the vignette says the map goes on; only "You are here" and its next topics must be whole, and a map is never shrunk or moved just to avoid a cut). Ranking cuts first had kept Poisson off Probability's course page and opened Linear Algebra on Home looking past its last layer, an empty band above Today (round 5). On an axis the course overflows, the opening view never looks past the drawing's room, so the frame never ends on an empty band. A card is cut when part of it shows, even faintly in the fade, and the frame's edge cuts the rest (`cardFit`); a card is whole when it is wholly in the frame (clear, or partly under the fade). The room around the drawing is in the frame's pixels at every scale, since the fade is drawn on the screen: scaling it with the drawing put the tag of a topic on the first layer into the fade below 100%, so the course page could only open at full size. Edo's 14" window, on the demo seed (`tests/topic-map-framing.test.ts`): every course opens on "You are here" with every topic it leads to whole, Probability's four on its course page too: they need 851px in a row at 80%, and the course page's map is 864px (page-frame.md; a 696px pane had cut Poisson). It returns to that view whenever the frame grows or shrinks. `fitsDown` sets a drawing shorter than the frame at its top instead of its middle (the course page, beside its list; a lesson's steps). `whole` shows the whole course at whatever scale fits it (the enlarged map's Whole course), and `onOpensWhole` tells the page whether there is more to see than the opening view.
- Whole course: a page whose map opens on less than the whole course offers the maps' one zoom out control (`whole-course-button.tsx`, `course-map-whole`): a quiet button, a Zoom out icon and the words "Whole course", which shows every topic and becomes "Back to where you are" with a Locate icon; `-` toggles it while it shows, unless a field has the key. The page holds the choice for the visit only. Home's enlarged map and the course page pass it as `whole` (split-course.md).
- Drag or scroll pans; a pinch (or Ctrl and scroll) zooms between the whole course, however small, and full size.
- Held still while it fits (Edo, 16 Nov 2026: "fixed without the user being able to drag it… when the graph itself cleanly fits on screen… The only navigation… zooming in… and zooming out"): while the camera shows the whole drawing (`showsWhole`), neither a drag nor a scroll pans, and a scroll is the page's. A pinch zooms in, to 150% (`maxZoom`, so a course that fits at full size still zooms), and only then does it pan, within the view that shows the drawing whole (`wholeExtent`, its `translateExtent`), so pinching back out lands on that view, centred. All React Flow's own props (`panOnDrag`, `panOnScroll`, `zoomOnScroll`, `preventScrolling`, `translateExtent`, `minZoom`, `maxZoom`). Probed at 1512×982 on the enlarged map, Probability (round 14, second pass): a drag moved the map 138 px and a scroll 30 px, a pinch could not zoom in, and a zoom left it 187 px off its view; now 0, 0, to 150% with pans while zoomed in, and back to 0 px off.
- Hover or focus lights a topic with its whole prerequisite chain: their lines rise to white 70% and every other topic recedes to 80% (`RECEDED`, 120ms); the other lines drop to 35%. 80% is the lowest strength at which a receded card's muted line keeps 4.5:1 on its card (WCAG AA), so every card on the map still reads; 50% drew it at 2.8:1 (round 5), and Edo ruled that only topics further than one step from "You are here" may dim, and not at all if dimming fails contrast (14 Nov 2026).
- `highlight` (optional, off by default): a set of topics a page lights from elsewhere, drawn as a hover draws a chain (the set's nodes and the lines between them lit, the other topics receded), except that "You are here" and the topics it leads to never recede while it lights (Edo, 14 Nov 2026: they stay whole and readable). A hover or focus takes over while it lasts and the highlight returns after it. The course page points at a topic's chain this way; Home lights its line's topics.
- `framed` (optional, off by default): topics a page points at, which the camera brings into view (`lit-camera.ts`). Once the page rests on them for 120ms, when they are not all clear of the fade, it glides in 200ms to frame them whole, as `scrollOn` brings a topic into a view, cutting as few cards at the frame's edge as it can and moving as little as it can, at its scale or as far out as the topics need to fit; "You are here" and its tag stay clear too when they fit with them. Once the page points at nothing for 400ms, or at topics this drawing does not hold, it glides back to the opening view. A pan, scroll or zoom of his own, or a keyboard walk, leaves the camera where he left it until the next point; after that point, leaving returns it as usual. Under reduced motion it moves at once. Off while the whole course shows. Home passes the topics of the Today row he points at (home-map.md), the course page the topic of the row he points at (split-course.md, Pointing).
- `marked` (optional, off by default): the one topic a page points at from elsewhere, so he sees at once which card it is (Edo, 14 Nov 2026). Its card wears `lh-card-mark` (`app/globals.css`): its border in the accent, a 1px accent ring and a soft 16px glow, held while it is marked; it blooms in once over 1.6s, a 2px ring and a 28px glow for the first second, then settles, with no loop, and fades out over 150ms when the mark leaves. Under reduced motion the settled ring shows at once. The course page marks the topic of the row he points at or arrows to.
- Keyboard: one tab stop, the current topic. Up goes to the nearest prerequisite, Down to the nearest topic that needs it, Left and Right along the layer; a topic reached out of view is brought into it (200ms). The focused node draws a 2px `ring` ring 2px outside its border (`focus-visible:ring-2`, offset on the background): an outline would never show, since the app's no-ring law in `globals.css` removes every outline. The drawing is a `figure` whose caption names the course, its topic count and where he is; each node is a link named by its title, "You are here", its standing in words and its line, and described by its prerequisites.
- Motion: none on open. The map is hidden only until its frame is measured, and its camera is placed before that frame paints, so every node shows at once, already framed (the round-3 evaluation saw nodes fade in 2.5s late, edges first). Zooming out to the whole course and back glides over 200ms; under reduced motion the camera moves at once.

## Arcs

Stacked arcs (Edo, 16 Nov 2026: "we keep them disconnected… instead of being able to freely scroll up and down, we have these fixed camera views… one clean simple up-down UI/UX in a very minimalist to-the-side kind of fashion… (and we can also have a keyboard shortcut for this) simply moves us up or down vertically with a fixed animation. It focuses the view and locks the view back to the current arc"; shipped 17 Nov 2026).

- **One arc at a time.** `arc` draws one arc of a course with arcs (`arc-view.ts`, `arcView`; design-v2.md 5.3; `drawingOf`): the arc's topics whole, and its entry topics (what it builds on from earlier arcs, the engine's `entry`) held in the top layer, each card receded (`RECEDED`) with its header "From <arc>" under a corner-up mark (`topic-from`, `data-entry`); a press on that line shows that arc, where the page can show one (canvas/canvas.md, Behaviour). Only the needs between drawn topics are drawn, none between two entry topics, and none on an entry topic that a longer path in the drawing already carries, so an entry card never pulls a topic away from the arc it builds on. Hover lights a chain inside the drawing only. Unset, `arc` draws the whole course: a course without arcs, and the enlarged map's and the course page's Whole course.
- **The arcs stack top to bottom in study order, disconnected:** no line runs between two arcs, and at rest only the shown arc is on the canvas (Edo: "It just shows that arc cleanly").
- **The glide.** When the arc shown changes, the arc it left stays drawn beside the new one, above it when it comes first and below when after, 160 apart on one vertical axis (`trailOf`, `ARC_GAP`), its cards out of the map's focus, keys and links (`trail-node.tsx`), and React Flow's own `setViewport` glides from where the camera was to the new arc's opening view in 420ms, eased in and out. The arc it left then goes. A jump of several arcs glides as one. Under reduced motion the new arc shows at once.
- **The opening view** is the map's (Behaviour, above): the whole arc when it fits at 80% or more, else framed on "You are here" with the topics it leads to whole. Wheel and drag never leave the arc: the camera's bounds are the arc's own (`wholeExtent`), opened wide only for the glide.
- **The arc list** (`../arc-map.tsx`, `ArcMap`, the one component every map with arcs is drawn in: Home, the enlarged map and the course page; `../arc-index.tsx`, `data-slot` `arc-index`; Edo, 17 Nov 2026: "the graph should be the same single standardised component across views"): every arc of the course top to bottom in study order, in a column of its own left of the map, each its number and whole name in quiet text (`ArcLabel`, no pill, no count), each a press that glides there. The rows hang off a hairline track, the course tabs' underline on its side, so arcs read as parts of the course; a bar on the track slides to the arc shown, and a dot in "You are here"'s ring ink marks the arc he is in. Its rows never move, whichever arc shows (Edo, 16 Nov 2026: "plenty of layout shift… I'm not able to orient myself in it", of the selector it replaced). The map frames each arc in the room beside it.
- **The keys** (`../arc-keys.ts`, the cheat sheet's Home rows; the course page's map takes them too): ⇧↑ and ⇧↓ glide to the arc above and below, stopping at the first and last; ⌘J goes to "You are here" from any arc, gliding to its arc and focusing its card, and leaves Whole course, from anywhere on the page, a field included (a ⌘ chord, so it never takes a letter from a composer; Edo, 17 Nov 2026). The arrows wait while a field has the key or something is open over it; Home's own keys take plain arrows, f, o, c and -.
- **The course page** draws the same arc list left of its map pane; an arc shown there opens in the page's own list of arcs (split-course.md).

`ArcPill` (`../arc-pill.md`) is how an arc is named anywhere else (the course head, What next, the topic head), never as "Course · Arc" text.
