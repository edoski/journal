# Learning memory and retrieval: 2026 evidence

> Historical investigation: implementation descriptions and proposals below reflect an earlier snapshot and are not the current runtime contract. See the [current learning README](../../../learning/README.md) and [behavioral acceptance cases](../behavioral-acceptance.md). Research claims retain their stated dates and limitations.

Checked 2026-09-18. Scope: one learner, shared local Obsidian records, native Claude/Codex conversations and Pi. Research only; no implementation or paid model evaluation.

## Verdict

Current evidence supports preserving recoverable evidence, distinguishing current state from historical observations, and selecting context according to the task. It does not establish a universal winner between long context, retrieval and compressed memory, or justify an arbitrary three-observation limit. For this small learning store, begin with useful selected-topic history and ordinary local reads; add finer retrieval only when size or measured failures justify it.

## Findings

1. **Retrieval breadth is task-dependent.** *Harness the Memory* (arXiv v1, **2026-08-15**) compares memory representations under three backbones. Increasing retrieval depth improved factual conversation QA but reduced success on a sequential action benchmark. The experiments use Qwen3/Gemma backbones, benchmark-specific retrieval ranges and a common model judge; they do not test tutoring or this system's frontier models. Some implementations differ from the original systems, and the paper promises code upon acceptance rather than supplying a demonstrated production recipe. **Design inference:** load enough selected-topic observations to reconstruct learning history; keep unrelated courses out. A lesson-history question and resuming an unfinished exercise need different evidence. Neither “always retrieve less” nor “always load everything” follows. [Paper and full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15008v1); [submission date](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15008).

2. **Compression loss and retrieval failure require different fixes.** *Does Your Agent's Memory Survive a Model Upgrade?* (arXiv v1, **2026-09-04**, under review) studies 48 scripted histories and two similarly sized models. Its diagnostics attribute most tested note-memory loss to store construction, while retrieval misses dominate its simple dense-RAG pipeline. Rewriting surviving notes cannot recreate omitted evidence; raw-history repair succeeds in only one tested migration direction. Histories fit both models' windows, and the pooled writer-swap penalty does not meet the paper's prespecified five-point threshold: it does **not** prove summaries universally fail across models. **Design inference:** keep dated observations and recoverable study artifacts behind editable summaries. Share plain records and source links across hosts; avoid assuming that prose written by one host is a complete evidence source for another. [Paper and full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05339v1); [submission date](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05339).

3. **Current truth is different from remembering an old fact.** *Can Agent Memory Systems Track Evolving State?* (arXiv v1, **2026-08-20**) tests superseded facts and dependent conclusions. Explicit state handling improves results in its benchmark, but the authors acknowledge synthetic scenarios, short histories, single runs and untested frontier answerers. Their full method requires roughly **165–600 encoder calls per scenario**. Those costs rule out copying the architecture by default here. **Design inference:** preserve historical learning observations, but distinguish them from the latest assessment, corrected preferences and current task. A later successful attempt may update the assessment without erasing earlier difficulty; dates alone do not express every correction or dependency. This is a record-design recommendation, not evidence that a separate state-extraction model is needed. [Paper and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19652v1); [submission date](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19652).

4. **Memory machinery must earn its total cost.** *Total Recall at What Cost?* (arXiv v1, **2026-08-12**) compares three memory systems with rolling-window and full-history references through 400 turns. Break-even depends on system, backbone and workload; some configurations never become cheaper within the tested range. Accuracy spans 21–54% on its LoCoMo subset. Limitations include synthetic cost dialogues, one accuracy corpus, imperfect cost fits and an uncontrolled ingest-backbone setting for one system. These API-cost results do not directly price subscription-backed Claude/Codex/Pi, but token use, latency and extra calls still matter. **Design inference:** first use topic identifiers, local paths and ordinary text search. Require a demonstrated recall or continuity improvement before adding embeddings, rerankers or separate model calls. [Paper and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11879v1); [submission date](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11879).

5. **Current lab practice favors selective loading and simpler instructions.** Anthropic's **2026-07-24** engineering article reports removing over 80% of Claude Code's system prompt without measurable loss on its coding evaluations, and recommends progressive disclosure of skills and references. This is a first-party engineering report, not a controlled independent tutoring study; its particular model claims should not be generalized across hosts. **Design inference:** keep the shared learning skill focused on durable teaching behavior and the local interface. Load course material, observations and session artifacts when relevant rather than embedding their contents in permanent instructions. Progressive disclosure need not mean a vector database: topic selection and file reads implement the basic pattern. [Dated official article](https://claude.com/blog/the-new-rules-of-context-engineering-for-claude-5-generation-models).

6. **External memory can remain application-owned files.** Anthropic's current memory-tool documentation is **undated**, inspected **2026-09-18**; its API type's 2025 suffix is not a 2026 publication date. It specifies client-side file operations, application-controlled storage, ranged reads and reuse of the same store across conversations. It distinguishes durable memory from server-side conversation compaction. This documents capability, not comparative effectiveness, and does not imply Claude Desktop automatically shares arbitrary files with Codex or Pi. **Design inference:** retain the existing local learning core as the shared owner, with each host accessing it through its supported tools. An additional memory service is unnecessary merely to persist records. [Current official API documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/memory-tool).

## Application to the current learning core

The current [README](../../../learning/README.md) describes summaries, append-only observations, active work and recoverable sessions, but defaults context to the latest three observations per focused topic. The following are proposed choices, not implemented changes or experimentally established optima.

| Situation | Context to provide | Additional machinery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary study of a selected topic | Current goal, topic summary, useful retained observations, unfinished work and relevant source references | Existing local reads |
| Resuming an exercise | Exact task, learner's latest attempt, feedback, completed steps and next unresolved step | Load its saved artifact when needed |
| Comparing progress across sessions | Relevant earlier and later observations, including contrary evidence | Broader topic history; no fixed three-item cap |
| A genuinely large topic history | Current state plus selected evidence; expose what was omitted and how to retrieve it | Start with topic/date/path/text filtering and paging |
| Repeated failures to locate semantically relevant evidence | Compare richer search against the simple baseline on real failed cases | Embeddings or model-assisted selection only after gains justify total cost |

Summaries are navigation aids and provisional assessments. Source observations should remain available for checking an old mistake, changed understanding or an overconfident summary. Avoid repeatedly summarizing summaries as the only surviving history.

A useful context check is whether the next host can recover the actual unfinished exercise, identify a corrected misconception, distinguish old from current preferences and find the supporting source. Measure omitted evidence and latency before tuning a token budget. No source here establishes a universal token threshold or optimal observation count.

## Confidence

High confidence in the source dates and the documented application-owned file interface. Moderate confidence in the general tradeoffs: independent 2026 preprints identify different failure mechanisms, but the reported magnitudes belong to their tested settings. Low confidence in any claim of a universal state of the art or direct improvement for this learner: the studies do not evaluate this exact Claude/Codex/Pi tutoring workflow.

## Gaps

No inspected study measures long-term learning outcomes for one student using a shared Obsidian memory across these hosts. No evidence determines when this store becomes large enough to need finer retrieval, or whether an added embedding/model stage would outperform complete selected-topic reads at acceptable cost. Real usage cases and their omitted evidence would settle those local questions better than importing a benchmark leaderboard.

All four research papers above were first submitted in 2026, with v1 the displayed version at inspection. Older papers surfaced during search were excluded; a 2026 conference label or recent crawl alone did not qualify a 2025 paper. The dated engineering article and undated API documentation are explicitly separate from research evidence.
